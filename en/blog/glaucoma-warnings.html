Glaucoma

Glaucoma — the silent thief of sight, what everyone over 40 should know

Glaucoma is irreversible optic-nerve disease — peripheral vision is lost before central acuity. This page covers acute glaucoma emergencies, who needs annual screening, and a preview of treatment options.

🚨 Red flags — go to ER immediately

Sudden severe eye pain + redness + nausea/vomiting + halos around lights
Acute angle-closure glaucoma — irreversible blindness within 24 hours, ER now!
Sudden tunnel-vision narrowing
End-stage glaucoma or acute optic nerve injury — see ophthalmology immediately
Sudden unilateral vision blur
Rule out acute glaucoma / central retinal artery occlusion

Principle: when in doubt, treat as emergency. The eye is an irreversible organ — the cost of delay far outweighs the inconvenience of an ER visit.

When should you see an ophthalmologist?

  • Annual IOP + visual field + OCT after age 40
  • Family history (first-degree relative with glaucoma)
  • High myopia > −6.00 D
  • Diabetes, long-term steroid users
  • Frequent headaches + halos around lights
