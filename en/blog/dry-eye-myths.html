8 Dry-Eye Myths
Do artificial tears make eyes drier? Are warm compresses pseudoscience?

Management of dryness, irritation, or tearing depends on the cause of dry eye and how eye drops are used. This guide begins with common questions about artificial tears, warm compresses, and lutein, then explains practical precautions and care at home. Persistent redness, irritation, or vision changes still need an ophthalmologist’s assessment.

13 min readLast updated · 2026-10-16Dr. Hsiao →
Full summary and research highlights

Dry eye disease (DED) is one of the most-questioned conditions in clinic — "do artificial tears make my eyes drier?", "does lutein cure it?", "do warm compresses actually work?". Most DED is predominantly evaporative (often linked to meibomian gland dysfunction, MGD; TFOS DEWS III notes that at least two-thirds is evaporative) — it's not just insufficient tear volume. With frequent, long-term use, what can harm the ocular surface is often not the tear drops themselves, but the BAK preservative in some bottled products. This article debunks the 8 most common misconceptions and offers 5 evidence-based self-care steps.

⚠️ Disclaimer: This is general medical education, not individual medical advice. For persistent redness, irritation, or vision changes, consult an ophthalmologist for full evaluation including Schirmer test, TBUT, and meibography.

迷思 1 · 「人工淚液越點越乾、會依賴」

迷思：點久了眼睛會習慣，自己分泌的淚水會越來越少，變成不點不行。
真相：對的成分不會。會讓眼睛越來越乾的，是人工淚液裡的「BAK 防腐劑」，不是淚液本身。

BAK 是氯化苯二甲烴銨（benzalkonium chloride）的縮寫，是多劑型瓶裝眼藥水常用的防腐劑。TFOS DEWS II 與 2025 年 TFOS DEWS III 報告都指出：BAK 等防腐劑對眼表有毒性並會引發發炎，可能破壞淚膜穩定、傷害角膜神經，反而讓乾眼的徵象與症狀惡化。

真正的「依賴感」其實來自兩件事：（1）患者本身的瞼板腺機能障礙（MGD）持續惡化，讓淚膜越來越不穩定，自然越點越要點；（2）反覆使用含 BAK 滴劑造成的醫源性傷害。

解法很簡單：美國眼科醫學會 2023 年版乾眼症臨床指引（AAO Preferred Practice Pattern，PPP）建議，若一天點超過 4 次或長期頻繁使用，一般改用無防腐劑（preservative-free）人工淚液，例如單支裝，或 ABAK、COMOD 等設計成防止汙染的無防腐劑多劑型瓶。這類產品不含防腐劑，可依需要使用，不會有防腐劑累積的毒性。

迷思 2 · 「眼睛狂流淚就一定不是乾眼」

迷思：我每天眼睛都會流淚，看電腦也流、吹風也流 — 所以我不可能是乾眼。
真相：流淚本身就是乾眼的常見症狀之一（AAO 乾眼症 PPP 把流淚列為乾眼症狀），常是眼表受刺激後的反射性流淚（reflex tearing）。

蒸發型乾眼（evaporative dry eye）是最常見的類型：TFOS DEWS III 指出至少三分之二的乾眼屬蒸發型；一項門診研究中，約 86% 的乾眼病人有瞼板腺機能障礙（MGD）的跡象，單純淚液分泌不足的只占約 14%（Lemp 2012）。MGD 讓淚膜油脂層不足，淚水蒸發太快。眼表面持續受到刺激會啟動三叉神經反射，讓淚腺大量分泌水樣的反射性淚水。

但這種「反射性淚水」以水分為主，補不了不足的油脂層，只能短暫覆蓋眼表面，很快又蒸發。所以患者會出現一個矛盾的情況：一邊狂流淚，一邊覺得乾、刺、糊。

診斷不能只靠「我會不會流淚」。TFOS DEWS III（2025）的診斷要件是：症狀問卷（OSDI-6）陽性，再加上至少一項徵象：非侵入式淚膜破裂時間 < 10 秒、淚液滲透壓升高，或眼表染色。接著再用淚河高度、Schirmer 氏試驗（淚液分泌量）、瞼板腺攝影（腺體萎縮程度）等檢查找出病因，分清是淚液分泌不足（水液缺乏型）還是蒸發型，治療方向不同。

迷思 3 · 「乾眼症是老人病，年輕人不會」

迷思：我才 28 歲，乾眼症應該是老人或更年期才會得吧？
真相：年輕人也會乾眼。TFOS DEWS III 整理的流行病學資料顯示，以症狀與徵象定義時，年輕成人與年長者的比率都偏高，且有證據顯示乾眼盛行率隨時間上升。

TFOS DEWS III 指出，看螢幕時眨眼減少、配戴隱形眼鏡等都和乾眼有關。年輕人常見的三大推手：

  • 3C 螢幕使用 — 看螢幕時眨眼次數明顯減少，而且常常是「不完全眨眼」（incomplete blinking），造成淚膜不穩定、蒸發增加（TFOS Lifestyle 數位環境報告、TFOS DEWS III）；不完全眨眼也和瞼板腺萎縮、油脂品質變差有關（AAO PPP）。
  • 軟式隱形眼鏡 — 多達一半的隱形眼鏡使用者有配戴不適（contact lens discomfort），乾澀是常見症狀（TFOS 隱形眼鏡不適國際工作坊，2013）。
  • 自體免疫疾病 — 修格蘭氏症候群（Sjögren's syndrome）、全身性紅斑性狼瘡（SLE）、類風濕性關節炎（RA）可能以乾眼、口乾、關節痛等表現。AAO PPP 提醒：乾眼最常見於停經後女性，若發生在年輕人或男性，應留意是否有全身性或局部的相關疾病。

如果您 30 歲以下、長時間用螢幕、戴隱眼，還抱怨眼睛乾澀刺痛 — 不是錯覺，該看就看。

迷思 4 · 「葉黃素吃了就能治乾眼」

迷思：藥局店員推薦葉黃素護眼，應該對乾眼也有效？
真相：葉黃素主要用在黃斑部病變（AMD）的營養配方，單獨用來治乾眼的證據很少；Omega-3 是較常被研究的補充品，但結果並不一致。

葉黃素（lutein）與玉米黃素（zeaxanthin）是黃斑部色素的主要組成。相關證據主要來自 AREDS2 研究：4203 位有惡化為晚期年齡相關性黃斑部病變（AMD）風險的病人中，在原本的 AREDS 配方加入葉黃素與玉米黃素，主要分析並未進一步降低惡化風險；但因 β-胡蘿蔔素可能增加曾吸菸者的肺癌風險，研究者認為葉黃素與玉米黃素適合取代配方中的 β-胡蘿蔔素。這些都是 AMD 的研究，跟乾眼症是兩回事。

對乾眼症有理論基礎的營養補充是 Omega-3 多元不飽和脂肪酸（EPA／DHA）——理論上可減輕眼表發炎、改善瞼板腺油脂品質。但 2018 年發表在 NEJM 的 DREAM 試驗（535 人）結果是：每天 3000 mg Omega-3 與橄欖油安慰劑相比，12 個月內乾眼症狀分數（OSDI）的改善沒有顯著差異，淚膜破裂時間、Schirmer 氏試驗與染色分數也沒有差異。

因此 AAO 2023 年版乾眼症 PPP 對口服 Omega-3 的建議是「可斟酌使用」（discretionary）——意思是「想試可以試，但別把它當主治療」；TFOS DEWS III 也認為 Omega-3 在乾眼治療的角色仍不確定。至於葉黃素，DEWS III 只提到含葉黃素、玉米黃素、薑黃素與維生素 D 的複方補充品在 2 個隨機試驗中改善淚液量與穩定度，證據仍有限，不能說「吃葉黃素就能治乾眼」。

迷思 5 · 「點一陣子藥就會痊癒」

迷思：醫生開的藥水點一個月，應該就能根治了吧？
真相：乾眼症是慢性病，類似高血壓、糖尿病，需要長期管理與生活型態調整，停藥常會復發。

TFOS DEWS III（2025）對乾眼症的定義是：「多因子、有症狀的疾病，特徵是淚膜和（或）眼表恆定性喪失；淚膜不穩定與高滲透壓、眼表發炎與損傷，以及神經感覺異常都是病因。」AAO 乾眼症 PPP 也強調，要讓病人了解乾眼的慢性病程——它不是急性感染可以「一個療程治癒」，而是要像高血壓、糖尿病一樣長期管理。

迷思 6 · 「人工淚液都差不多，選便宜就好」

迷思：藥局架上幾十種人工淚液，反正都是「水」，挑便宜的就好。
真相：成分、防腐劑、滲透壓、黏度都不同。選錯可能效果不佳；若長期頻繁使用含防腐劑的產品，還可能傷害眼表。

但比成分更關鍵的是防腐劑。多劑型瓶裝可能含 BAK；也有產品改用較溫和的防腐劑（如 Purite、sodium perborate、Polyquad），或採用防止汙染的無防腐劑多劑型瓶（TFOS DEWS III）。含 BAK 的產品長期高頻次使用，可能累積對眼表的毒性。

選購原則

  • 輕度乾眼、眼表健康，且一天 ≤ 4 次 → 含防腐劑的多劑型瓶裝通常可以
  • 一天 > 4 次，或長期頻繁使用 → 一般建議無防腐劑（單支裝，或 ABAK、COMOD 等無防腐劑多劑型瓶）（AAO PPP）
  • 戴隱形眼鏡時，請選標示「適用於配戴隱形眼鏡」的低防腐配方
  • 有眼藥水過敏史（癢、紅、刺）→ 直接選無防腐劑

迷思 7 · 「熱敷沒效，只是心理作用」

迷思：熱敷只是覺得舒服，沒什麼科學根據，還不如直接點藥水。
真相：熱敷是 MGD 常用的居家治療，但溫度和時間要夠（TFOS DEWS III：約 40–41.5°C，每天 1 次 10 分鐘或 2 次各 5 分鐘），之後再輕柔按壓眼瞼，才能把變稠的油脂推出來。

阻塞的瞼板腺（meibomian gland）分泌物比正常的更黏稠、熔點更高：正常油脂約 32°C 開始熔化，阻塞型 MGD 約 35°C 才開始（MGD 國際工作坊，2011）。熱敷就是要把眼瞼加熱到 40°C 左右，讓變稠的油脂軟化、比較容易排出；冷敷或常溫敷無法提高眼瞼溫度，對 MGD 幫助有限。

有效熱敷的三條件：

  1. 溫度約 40–41.5°C — TFOS DEWS III 建議的加熱溫度；過熱可能燙傷眼瞼皮膚，溫度不夠則軟化不了油脂。
  2. 每天 1 次 10 分鐘，或每天 2 次各 5 分鐘 — 熱度要維持夠久才有效；研究發現熱敷至少約 4 分鐘，眼瞼內側溫度才能達到 40°C 以上（Blackie 2008）。
  3. 輕柔按壓 — 熱敷完立刻做瞼板腺按摩（由眼皮根部往睫毛方向推），把軟化的油脂推出腺體開口。

市售熱敷產品很多，但不是每一種都有安全與療效的證據，加熱方式不同，效果也可能不同（TFOS DEWS III）。用溫毛巾也可以：研究中每 2 分鐘換一次加熱到 45°C 的毛巾，傳熱效果與市售產品相當，只是比較費工。需要時也可在診所做脈衝光（IPL）或 LipiFlow 熱脈動治療。

迷思 8 · 「乾眼症不會傷眼睛，忍一忍就好」

迷思：乾眼又不是青光眼、白內障，不會傷視力，忍一忍就過去。
真相：輕度確實沒事；但嚴重乾眼可能造成角膜上皮缺損、絲狀角膜炎、潰瘍，嚴重時可能穿孔失明。

輕度乾眼是討厭但沒事；嚴重乾眼（尤其是修格蘭氏症候群、全身性紅斑性狼瘡，或造血幹細胞移植後出現移植物對抗宿主疾病（GVHD）的族群）則真的會出事。臨床上常見的併發症：

  • 絲狀角膜炎（filamentary keratitis） — 黏液絲附著在角膜表面，每眨眼都拉扯，疼痛與異物感明顯。
  • 持續性角膜上皮缺損（persistent epithelial defect，PED） — 角膜破皮超過 10–14 天仍無法癒合，容易繼發感染、潰瘍甚至穿孔（Vaidyanathan 2019）。
  • 角膜潰瘍（corneal ulcer） — 可能是感染性或無菌性（AAO PPP）；感染時需要積極的抗生素治療。
  • 角膜融解（corneal melt） — 嚴重乾眼合併全身性發炎疾病（如自體免疫疾病）時可能出現，可能穿孔致失明，屬於急症。

所以反覆出現「眼睛紅、刺痛、視力下降」絕不要拖。該做就做 Schirmer 氏試驗、TBUT 與眼表染色（螢光素、lissamine green 或孟加拉紅），該轉醫學中心就轉。

Myth 1 · "Artificial tears make your eyes drier; you'll become dependent on them"

Myth: The longer you use them, the less your eyes produce on their own; eventually you can't stop.
Truth: Not the tears themselves — it's the BAK preservative inside that damages eyes long-term.

BAK (benzalkonium chloride) is a common preservative in multi-dose eye-drop bottles. The TFOS DEWS II and 2025 TFOS DEWS III reports both note that preservatives such as BAK have toxic and pro-inflammatory effects on the ocular surface — they can destabilize the tear film and impair corneal nerves, worsening dry eye signs and symptoms.

The "dependency" feeling actually comes from two sources: (1) the patient's underlying meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD) progressively worsens, making the tear film increasingly unstable; (2) iatrogenic injury from chronic BAK use.

The fix is simple: the AAO 2023 Dry Eye Syndrome Preferred Practice Pattern (PPP) generally recommends preservative-free tears when drops are used frequently and chronically (e.g., more than 4 times a day) — single-dose vials, or multi-dose preservative-free bottles designed to prevent contamination (such as ABAK or COMOD). Without preservative, they can be used as often as needed without preservative toxicity accumulating.

Myth 2 · "Watery eyes can't be dry eye"

Myth: My eyes water all day — when I look at screens, when wind blows — so I can't possibly have dry eye.
Truth: Tearing is itself a common dry eye symptom (the AAO Dry Eye PPP lists it among dry eye symptoms) — often reflex tearing from an irritated ocular surface.

Evaporative dry eye is the most common form: TFOS DEWS III notes that at least two-thirds of dry eye is evaporative, and in one clinic-based study about 86% of dry eye patients showed signs of meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD), with only about 14% purely aqueous-deficient (Lemp 2012). MGD leads to insufficient lipid in the tear film and faster evaporation. Persistent ocular-surface irritation activates a trigeminal reflex that makes the lacrimal gland release watery reflex tears.

But this reflex tear is mostly water and cannot replace the deficient lipid layer, so it only briefly covers the surface before evaporating. So the patient experiences a paradox: watering and dryness/burning/blurriness at the same time.

Diagnosis cannot rest on "do you tear?" alone. Under TFOS DEWS III (2025), diagnosis requires a positive symptom screen (OSDI-6) plus at least one sign: non-invasive tear break-up time <10 s, tear hyperosmolarity, or ocular surface staining. Tests such as tear meniscus height, Schirmer (tear secretion), and meibography (gland atrophy) then help identify the cause — aqueous-deficient vs evaporative — and treatment differs.

Myth 3 · "Dry eye is a senior disease; the young don't get it"

Myth: I'm only 28 — dry eye is for the elderly or postmenopausal women, right?
Truth: Young people get dry eye too. Epidemiology summarized in TFOS DEWS III shows that, defined by symptoms and signs, rates are higher in both younger and older adults, with some evidence that prevalence is increasing over time.

TFOS DEWS III links reduced blinking during screen use and contact lens wear to dry eye. Three common drivers in young people:

  • Screen use — blinking becomes much less frequent and often incomplete, causing tear film instability and increased evaporation (TFOS Lifestyle digital environment report, TFOS DEWS III); incomplete blinking is also associated with meibomian gland dropout and poorer meibum quality (AAO PPP).
  • Soft contact lenses — up to half of contact lens wearers experience contact lens discomfort, with dryness a common symptom (TFOS International Workshop on Contact Lens Discomfort, 2013).
  • Autoimmune disease — Sjögren's syndrome, SLE, and RA may present with dry eye, dry mouth, or joint pain. The AAO PPP notes that dry eye is most common in postmenopausal women, so dry eye in younger patients or men should prompt a search for associated systemic or local conditions.

If you're under 30, work long screen hours, wear contacts, and complain of burning/grittiness — it isn't your imagination. See an ophthalmologist.

Myth 4 · "Lutein cures dry eye"

Myth: The pharmacy clerk recommended lutein for "eye health" — that should help my dry eye, right?
Truth: Lutein's role is in AMD supplement formulas; evidence for lutein alone in dry eye is scant. Omega-3 is one of the more studied supplements, but results are inconsistent.

Lutein and zeaxanthin are the principal carotenoids of the macular pigment. The key evidence comes from the AREDS2 study: in 4,203 people at risk of progression to advanced age-related macular degeneration (AMD), adding lutein + zeaxanthin to the AREDS formulation did not further reduce progression in the primary analysis, but because beta carotene may raise lung cancer risk in former smokers, lutein + zeaxanthin was considered an appropriate substitute for it. That is AMD research — it has nothing to do with dry eye.

The supplement with theoretical basis for dry eye is omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids (EPA / DHA) — modulating the inflammatory cascade and improving meibum fluidity. But the 2018 NEJM DREAM trial (n=535) found no significant difference between 3000 mg/day omega-3 and olive oil placebo over 12 months in symptoms (OSDI) or signs (tear break-up time, Schirmer, staining).

Accordingly, the AAO 2023 Dry Eye Syndrome PPP rates oral omega-3 as discretionary — try if you wish, but it's not primary therapy — and TFOS DEWS III considers its role in dry eye uncertain. As for lutein, DEWS III only notes that a combination supplement (lutein, zeaxanthin, curcumin, vitamin D) improved tear volume and stability in 2 randomized trials; evidence is still limited, so lutein cannot be said to treat dry eye.

Myth 5 · "A short course of medication will cure it"

Myth: I'll take the prescribed drops for a month and be cured.
Truth: Dry eye is a chronic disease — like hypertension or diabetes. Long-term management is required; recurrence on stopping is normal.

TFOS DEWS III (2025) defines dry eye as "a multifactorial, symptomatic disease characterized by a loss of homeostasis of the tear film and/or ocular surface, in which tear film instability and hyperosmolarity, ocular surface inflammation and damage, and neurosensory abnormalities are etiological factors." The AAO Dry Eye PPP stresses educating patients about its chronic nature — not an acute infection cured in one course; it requires long-term management like blood pressure or blood sugar.

Myth 6 · "All artificial tears are basically the same — pick the cheapest"

Myth: There are dozens of brands but they're all basically water — just buy the cheapest.
Truth: Composition, preservative, osmolarity, and viscosity all differ. The wrong choice may work poorly, and frequent long-term use of preserved drops can harm the ocular surface.

But the bigger lever is the preservative. Multi-dose bottles may contain BAK; others use gentler preservatives (e.g., Purite, sodium perborate, Polyquad) or contamination-resistant preservative-free multi-dose bottles (TFOS DEWS III). High-frequency long-term use of BAK-containing drops can accumulate ocular surface toxicity.

Selection rules

  • Mild dry eye, healthy ocular surface, ≤4 uses/day → preserved multi-dose is usually fine
  • >4 uses/day, or frequent long-term use → preservative-free generally recommended (single-dose, or preservative-free multi-dose such as ABAK, COMOD) (AAO PPP)
  • Contact lens wearers — choose CL-compatible low-preservative formulations
  • History of eye-drop allergy (itch, redness, sting) → preservative-free directly

Myth 7 · "Warm compresses don't work; it's just placebo"

Myth: Warm compresses just feel nice; no real evidence — better to skip them and use drops.
Truth: They are a standard at-home treatment for MGD — but temperature and time matter (TFOS DEWS III: about 40–41.5°C, once daily for 10 min or twice daily for 5 min), followed by gentle lid massage to express the thickened meibum.

Secretions from obstructed meibomian glands are thicker, with higher melting points: normal meibum begins to melt at about 32°C, obstructive MGD meibum at about 35°C (International Workshop on MGD, 2011). Warm compresses aim to heat the lids to around 40°C so the thickened meibum softens and drains; cold or room-temperature compresses can't raise lid temperature and do little for MGD.

Three conditions for an effective warm compress:

  1. Temperature about 40–41.5°C — as recommended by TFOS DEWS III; too hot risks burning the lid skin, too low fails to soften meibum.
  2. Once daily for 10 min, or twice daily for 5 min — heat must be sustained; one study found at least about 4 minutes was needed for the inner lid to reach ≥40°C (Blackie 2008).
  3. Gentle massage — immediately after the compress, roll-press from lid base toward the lash margin to express the softened meibum.

Many commercial warming products exist, but not all have evidence for safety and efficacy, and different heating methods may not be equally effective (TFOS DEWS III). Warm towels work too: towels reheated to 45°C every 2 minutes delivered heat comparably to commercial devices, but it is labor-intensive. In-clinic IPL (intense pulsed light) or LipiFlow thermal pulsation are further options.

Myth 8 · "Dry eye doesn't damage eyes — just bear it"

Myth: It's not glaucoma or cataract — it doesn't hurt vision, just live with it.
Truth: Mild — yes, harmless. Severe dry eye can cause corneal epithelial defects, filamentary keratitis, ulcers, even perforation and blindness.

Mild dry eye is annoying but harmless. Severe dry eye — especially in Sjögren's, SLE, or GVHD after hematopoietic stem cell transplant — can cause:

  • Filamentary keratitis — mucin filaments adherent to cornea; each blink tugs them, severe pain and foreign-body sensation.
  • Persistent epithelial defect (PED) — an epithelial defect that fails to heal within 10–14 days, prone to infection, ulceration, even perforation (Vaidyanathan 2019).
  • Corneal ulcer — may be microbial or sterile (AAO PPP); infectious ulcers need aggressive antibiotic treatment.
  • Corneal melt — can occur in severe dry eye with systemic inflammatory (e.g., autoimmune) disease; can perforate and cause blindness — an emergency.

So recurrent redness, pain, or vision loss should never be ignored. Get Schirmer + TBUT + corneal staining (fluorescein / lissamine green / rose bengal) without delay; tertiary referral if needed.
